### Abstract

During the formative decade of digital journalism in India, regional-language news portals were constrained by non-standardized digital typography. In the Kannada media ecosystem, early web publishing relied on proprietary ASCII- and ISCII-remapped font glyphs (such as Baraha, Nudi, Akruti, and Shree-Lipi). Under this legacy paradigm, digital publishing functioned as visual typesetting rather than semantic computing: English keyboard character codes were mapped to visual Kannada glyph fragments. While human readers could view Kannada text if the corresponding proprietary font was installed locally or embedded via dynamic font objects, the underlying Document Object Model (DOM) contained arbitrary, unindexed Latin strings. Consequently, search engine crawlers, database systems, and operating systems processed this editorial content as unintelligible *mojibake*. Kannada digital news was largely isolated from the semantic web, devoid of search discoverability, cross-platform portability, content syndication, and digital accessibility. The adoption of the universal Unicode standard—specifically UTF-8 encoding across the Kannada block (`U+0C80`–`U+0CFF`)—marked a transformative infrastructural shift. This paper investigates the transition from proprietary font encodings to Unicode in Kannada digital news, analyzing the structural and communicative advantages this standardization unlocked across regional news ecologies.

This inquiry is situated at the intersection of media infrastructure studies and regional platformisation. Drawing upon the infrastructural turn in communication scholarship (Star & Bowker, 2002; Plantin et al., 2018), technical character encodings are conceptualized as invisible media infrastructures that govern linguistic visibility, information circulation, and institutional longevity. The paper connects this lens with theories of vernacular platformisation (Poell, Nieborg, & Duffy, 2022; Udupa, 2019), showing how modern platform ecosystems—search engines, social media feeds, and news aggregators—require standardized, machine-readable data structures to index and circulate vernacular discourse. Furthermore, the study engages with debates on the linguistic digital divide (Neyazi, 2018), asserting that semantic machine-readability is an essential prerequisite for equitable vernacular participation in platform-governed public spheres.

The research addresses three central questions:
1. What technical, architectural, and communicative bottlenecks characterized legacy ASCII font-encoded Kannada web journalism prior to Unicode standardization?
2. What structural and functional advantages did Unicode adoption introduce in platform interoperability, search engine indexing (SEO), database management, and cross-device rendering?
3. How has standardized machine-readable text facilitated the integration of Kannada digital newsrooms into platformized distribution ecologies, including Open Graph metadata protocols, RSS syndication, and algorithmic mobile news aggregation?

Methodologically, this study adopts a non-reactive empirical design combining longitudinal web archival analysis and digital source-code forensics. Rather than relying on subjective workplace interviews, the investigation conducts empirical audits of historical web artifacts preserved in the Internet Archive’s Wayback Machine across a twenty-four-year span (2000–2024). The empirical corpus comprises four major Kannada digital news portals representing legacy broadsheets with digital divisions (*Prajavani*, *Vijaya Karnataka*, and *Kannada Prabha*) and an early digital-native portal (*Oneindia Kannada / ThatsKannada*). Web archives are examined across three historical epochs: the Legacy Font Era (2000–2006), characterized by dynamic font downloads (`.eot`/`.ttf`) and static raster images; the Transitional Dual-Encoding Era (2007–2013), marked by parallel font-conversion scripts; and the Unicode-Native Era (2014–2024), defined by full UTF-8 compliance, responsive mobile frameworks, and semantic web metadata. The retrieved codebases and HTTP headers are evaluated against a five-pillar technical rubric: machine-readability and DOM text extraction, search engine crawlability (SEO), platform metadata integration (Open Graph and Twitter Cards), client-side font dependency elimination, and database storage durability.

The findings demonstrate five primary technical and structural advantages conferred by Unicode adoption:
1. **Universal Interoperability and Font Independence:** Unicode eliminated proprietary font dependencies, enabling native, cross-platform text rendering across Windows, macOS, Linux, Android, and iOS without client-side font installations. It established text portability, allowing readers to copy, paste, quote, and transfer Kannada text across software ecosystems without glyph disintegration.
2. **Search Engine Discoverability and Algorithmic Visibility:** By replacing unindexed ASCII glyph fragments with standardized hexadecimal character codes, search engine crawlers (Google, Bing) could parse native Kannada keywords in URL slugs, `<title>` tags, `<h1>` headers, and body text. This ended the digital invisibility of regional news and stimulated organic audience discovery.
3. **Platform Distribution and Syndication:** Unicode enabled seamless integration with social media platforms via Open Graph protocols, rendering clean Kannada preview cards across WhatsApp, Facebook, and X. It also enabled structured XML/RSS syndication feeds, facilitating automated ingestion by mobile news aggregators such as Dailyhunt and Google News.
4. **Computational Interactivity and Accessibility:** Machine-readable text unlocked full-text on-site search, automated content tagging, and participatory reader comments via standard system keyboards. Crucially, it enabled digital accessibility technologies, allowing screen readers and text-to-speech tools to parse Kannada journalism for visually impaired audiences.
5. **The Archival Paradox:** While Unicode secured the discoverability and storage stability of modern journalism, the empirical audit reveals that substantial volumes of pre-2008 articles remain locked in legacy font formats, creating an unindexed "archival black hole" that highlights the critical need for retrospective algorithmic transcoding.

This study demonstrates that vernacular platformisation is deeply contingent upon low-level technical standardization. By tracing how character encoding protocols enabled Kannada digital journalism to evolve from fragile desktop typesetting into an integrated, platform-ready news ecosystem, this paper offers a rigorous empirical foundation for understanding the infrastructural conditions of language and digital publics in the Global South.

**Keywords:** Unicode Standard, Kannada Digital Journalism, Media Infrastructure, Vernacular Platformisation, Search Engine Discoverability.
